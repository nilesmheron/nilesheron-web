01  ·  Summary
With no building to carry, the mission stands in the light.
Capital is the hook that brings everyone to the table. Equity is what the table produces.

Black Sand (a new working title for what was formerly the Center for Tech Equity) is a capital-led program to make Detroit the epicenter of early-stage venture for underrepresented founders and investors: the place founders from across the country come for their pre-seed through Series A capital, instead of San Francisco or New York; a place that becomes an anchor for Black and brown fund managers during their fundraising; and the place high-net-worth Black and brown investors look to invest in fund managers who are culturally aligned with them. It works by connecting, and developing, three communities into one coordinated continuum: limited partners, general partners, and founders, each with an established and an emerging cohort. When Black and brown LPs back Black and brown fund managers, those managers back Black and brown founders at rates the rest of the industry has never approached.

Without a building asset casting its time-cost shadow, all hours and dollars go toward what we're building, not where we're building it. That keeps the list of considerations short until the network demands a permanent home, and at that point a building becomes a catalyst, not a burden.

The program convenes in partner spaces across Detroit and in select locations across the country where there is strategic density of the communities we aim to develop and support. The sections that follow lay out the communities, the structure that holds them together, the operating model, the partners, the timeline from February 2027, what we'll track, and where the program goes in Year Two and beyond.

What is held firmly in this document is the what and the why: the North Star in Section 2 and the communities in Section 3. The how is a best effort on the information, landscape, and capacity available today. The programs in Sections 5 through 10 are real and executable as written, and they are also a first version. As resources, partners, and runway come into focus, there may be better tactical and practical strategy, better-defined target outcomes, and funders who find different parts of the vision compelling based on intermediate outcomes and deliverables. That is how this part of the work goes. The program is relentless about what it is trying to accomplish, and expects the how to keep improving.

02  ·  North Star and approach
Sand Hill Road, without the structural barriers and bias.
North Star. Detroit emerges as the most productive early-stage capital environment for underrepresented participants in venture, at every layer of the stack. For founders, it becomes the place where the pre-seed through Series A capital is, and where being Black or brown is an asset to the raise rather than a tax on it. For emerging fund managers, it becomes the place where LPs who won't discount your thesis for your culture are, and where a first fund closes faster because the room was built for it. For LPs, it becomes the place where capital can be deployed toward equitable innovation without sacrificing returns, with fund managers who understand where the future is headed as it pushes inward from the coasts. A funding environment that values culture and understands that capacity does not present in only one way.
Why the structure produces equity  ·  the evidence is consistent

The program does not treat equity as a separate workstream. It builds the capital stack so that equity is what the stack produces.
Approach. We don't rebuild what already works. We organize, amplify, and bridge: bringing established programs and capital to Detroit, connecting founders in secondary markets to coastal expertise at coastal speed, and filling gaps no one else fills. Our center of excellence is early-stage investment: preparing founders for capital, training emerging fund managers to deploy it, and connecting LPs who want to back them.

03  ·  Three communities
Connect every layer to the ones above and below it. Grow each one.
The program serves three communities, each with an established and an emerging subset. Together they form a capital continuum, and the program's job is to connect every layer to the ones above and below it, and to grow each one.
→ Established
Emerging →
Limited partners
the supply
Established LPs are endowments, foundations, and institutional allocators. For foundations in particular, this program offers something their grantmaking cannot: a way to deploy endowment capital responsibly, in funds whose performance is empirically indistinguishable from their peers,4 while producing the impact their missions call for.
Emerging LPs are Black, brown, women, and queer high-net-worth individuals, along with entertainers, athletes, and younger wealth holders who have not yet entered venture. Today their options are often limited to under-developed local pools or coastal deployment. This program gives them a third option: a Detroit-based path into minority-led and minority-forward funds nationally.
General partners
the distribution
Established GPs are managers mid-fund or actively raising, with the best deal flow and the most to teach. What brings them to the table is LP access; most of what else a program could offer them is available closer to home.
Emerging GPs are managers raising their first or second funds, often from under-resourced geographies and circumstances, often Black and brown, who bring an asymmetrical perspective on where value sits. They need the same LP access, and they seek value in both directions: up the chain to the LPs and established managers who can anchor a raise, and down the chain to the deal flow they have the highest need for and the least access to. Established GPs open that flow.

03  ·  Three communities, continued
Build the capital stack, and the capital finds the founders.
Founders
the outcome
Established founders, at Seed and Series A and beyond, validate the quality of the deal flow and anchor the network's credibility. Emerging founders are building companies in tertiary markets and should not be compelled to uproot in order to be funded. Founders are the reason the program exists, and the clearest evidence that the thesis holds: more founders of color raising more rounds through this network, year over year, is proof the system is working, whatever room for improvement remains. But the program has to start at the top of the ladder for it to work at all. General partners are required to distribute capital, which makes this one place where gravity does what it is supposed to: build the capital stack and the capital finds the founders. That gravity is what makes the thesis less trickle-down economics and more Ujamaa, Kwanzaa's principle of cooperative economics, with Black and brown capital building Black and brown businesses and profiting from them together. The work of preparing founders, and Detroit's Black founders specifically, to participate in that stack is real work, and it grows as a share of the program's effort every year the stack is operating.
The first task is to build this capital structure.
Then more and more, the work is helping founders of color prepare to use it.
The lift. For the established subset of every community, the lift is light. These are sophisticated participants who can reach their own goals once they are in the room together; what the program provides them is infrastructure to connect, light coaching, and community. The work is readying the emerging subsets to join their established peers, and it is necessary work on two dimensions at once: each emerging group needs to grow in quality, meaning velocity, precision, and network size, and in quantity, meaning more participants in the room.

04  ·  How the communities validate each other, and Year One
None of them will commit alone. Year One is casting before it is programming.

The three communities validate each other, and none of them will commit alone. A few LPs need to commit their supply. A few established GPs need to commit their validation. One or two promising emerging GPs need to raise with and through the platform. And there needs to be a batch of founders worth rallying behind, so that when the moment calls for it, there is a founder right here.

That makes Year One a casting exercise before it is a programming exercise. The first convenings are not public programming; they are rooms where the founding cast meets itself. The public moment comes once the cast is real enough to point at.

A structure like this needs an outside push to start, because the first commitment in each class has nothing yet to buy. Song Foundation's name and network, alongside Song United and Minority Capital, are that push for the LP and established GP classes. The program will build with firms that already operate on this thesis and span more than one class: 100KM Ventures in Washington, Black Ops VC in Oakland, MaC Venture Capital in Los Angeles, and South Loop Ventures in Houston. Together these are the first-push partners that stand up the continuum, bringing the LP relationships, deal flow, and validation that make the first conversations in Detroit non-speculative.

05  ·  Operating model
Two kinds of room do the work. Six rooms in Year One.
The program is built around convenings, with a minimal leasehold for key-personnel office space and no broader footprint until the network calls for one. Starting in Year One, two kinds of room do the work. Funder-to-funder convenings bring LPs and GPs together, established and emerging, so that LP access and deal flow move in the same room. Funder-to-founder convenings bring capital to a cohort and network of nationally competitive founders, with an open invitation to the best from Detroit and the expectation that network proximity will naturally preference them, when they are ready, without demanding that the local ecosystem singularly produce or even anchor the cohorts. The initial target is two of each in Year One, plus two more held in partnership with organizations like Venture 313, Transparent Collective, or MaC Venture Capital's Culture House, or alongside major convenings like SXSW, Art Basel, and CES, where the people we need are already in one place. Six rooms in Year One at the Base level of the budget in Section 11, with the expectation that demand and the first results push that number up as the cast fills in. Each one is closed-door and curated, held in partner spaces, and supported with travel for emerging GPs and founders so that geography is not the filter.

The sequence follows Section 3: the funder-to-funder rooms come first, because the stack has to exist before founders can be brought to it. Programs like the Cortex.AI House and the Midwest-to-coast exchange carry over from the earlier plan as the mechanism by which Detroit founders get coastal expertise at coastal speed. The opportunity for expansion is real on every axis: more convenings, more cities sending founders, and a wider set of partner firms as the model proves itself.

The whole founder. Capital without cultural embrace is not an acceptable outcome. Black and brown founders pursuing venture-scale companies face human barriers alongside financial ones: the weight of building in systems not designed to include them, navigating spaces that are psychologically costly, and often doing so without peer community or the space to build safely. Without a building, that commitment lives in the rooms we curate and the partners we choose rather than the walls we own. Every convening carries an explicit community component, and the emerging GP work carries the same principle, since many of those managers face the same barriers one layer up the stack.

Our commitment is to see and support the whole founder: the company builder and the human, joint and several.

06  ·  Partners and why Detroit
The hub is not an address. Partners in other cities send their founders here.
The program does not compete with the organizations already serving founders and managers. It organizes and amplifies them. Locally, that means TechTown and Venture 313, Femology, and Black Tech Saturdays. Nationally, it means New Majority Ventures, Transparent Collective in Oakland, Act House in Tulsa, Node.Miami in South Florida, and Cinematica Labs, the team behind Black Ambition's accelerator. These organizations are founder feed systems and programmatic partners. They host convenings, send founders, and co-design programming; the program partners with them and does not operate at their level. Transparent Collective and Black Ops VC remain the lead partners on founder preparation and deal advocacy, as in the earlier plan.

Detroit is the right epicenter for reasons that go deeper than geography. It has a vibrant philanthropic community, a history of industry, a density of university talent for the businesses that choose to stay, and a density of manufacturing infrastructure and domain knowledge that few cities can match. It also has a density of established Black and brown wealth, families and institutions whose capital has not yet flowed into venture and can be tapped as a new influx of LP supply. Michigan's and Detroit's foundations make it a particularly interesting place for GPs raising funds from institutional investors. For emerging LPs, Detroit is the third option between an under-developed local pool and coastal deployment. Detroit's own founders have an open invitation and the advantage of proximity, and the network will preference them as they are ready, without asking the local ecosystem to produce or anchor every cohort on its own.

It is also simply a good place to convene: in the middle of the country, a major hub airport, easy in and out from either coast and everywhere between, a downtown that is vibrant and still revitalizing, and a startup community with the resources to help develop what the convenings surface.

For founders in Oakland, Tulsa, Houston, and South Florida, Detroit is where the lead and follow capital for pre-seed through Series A rounds gathers in one room.

07  ·  Timeline
Cast it. Prove it. Raise on the proof.
The program clock starts February 2027, or sooner if practicable, with the fiduciary relationship with Detroit Future City in place before it does. Phases run in six-month blocks. Years One and Two run at the Base level of the budget in Section 11. The first joint raise opens at the end of Year One with a six-month window, and the programming it funds begins at the end of Year Two and runs through Year Three. Nothing in the phases below depends on money that has not landed. The programming the raise funds comes in the order GP, LP, then founder: founder programs already exist in number, so the program partners with them and contributes direction, advice, and expertise, and builds its own at full cost only once it is fully funded. Founder work goes on the whole time.

08  ·  What we'll track
We're not targeting activity.
We're targeting outcomes.
We will track both, but the goal is to wed ourselves to the impact we want to make, not just the deliverables we report to funders and partners, which can be performative. We commit to holding a firm internal impact dataset that tells us whether what we're doing is working, and to letting that be the organizational guide instead of activity.

The program will track from inception. Year One produces the data; Year Two carries the first targets, set on that data; Year Three carries targets and outcomes that are more aggressive because the first year earned them.

The long-run target: the combined capital of the LPs, GPs, and funds in the network, counted as AUM in the early years and as deployable capital in years four and five. Minority-led and minority-forward funds both count. The number is network-wide, not controlled; the program's job is to connect the capital, not to hold it.

09  ·  Thought leadership
The playbook is written in public.
The narrative that brings LPs and GPs to Detroit has to start before the results do, so distribution is a Year One question. The program will meaningfully investigate owned channels beyond any single partner: publishing, audio, social, and the convenings themselves as content, so that the story of what is happening in Detroit reaches the people who need to hear it without waiting for someone else to tell it. Alongside that, the rigorous work, the theory of change, the vertically integrated investment ecosystem model, and the data from Section 8, is where Detroit Future City's strength as a research and publishing partner comes in. Beginning in Phase 3, the program will co-produce a content series with DFC on Detroit's capital ecosystem. The purpose is practical: the playbook is written in public, and the national narrative that draws capital to Detroit is built from the program's own tracked results.
10  ·  Infrastructure and the longer horizon
A physical home, when it's time. Once the groundwork is done and the cast is real, the program will assess a physical home. No candidate is named here, and that is the point: the goal is to find the best home when the time is right, regardless of where it is. The criteria are the convening model's needs rather than a building's narrative: proximity to the partners who host now, capacity for the rooms the program actually uses, and terms that keep the program's capital flowing to the network rather than to rent.
Lightweight infrastructure. The program will look for opportunities to deploy lightweight infrastructural technology that increases the efficiency of the work: custom AI agents that help bind the network, and back-office solutions that a single GP, LP, or founder would never build for themselves. Because the program's win comes from the aggregate, it can approach that infrastructure differently than any one participant could. The first candidate is a concierge layer across the network: introductions, diligence routing, and expertise matching across all three communities, so that a founder in Tulsa and an LP in Detroit are one message apart.
Support built for each community. Beyond the rooms and the technology, the program will intentionally build the support each community needs most and is least likely to build for itself.
Emerging GPs
For emerging GPs, that is back-office support: fund administration, compliance, reporting, and the operating infrastructure that a first-time manager otherwise pays for out of a small management fee.
Founders
For founders, it is coaching and mentorship, drawn from the established founders and GPs already in the network.
LPs
For LPs, it is peer networks: a place where established and emerging allocators compare notes on managers, co-invest, and learn the asset class from each other rather than from the coasts.

11  ·  Budget
Each level is a complete program at its size. The levels nest.
The three-year budget accompanies this document as a live model with three levels. Each level is a complete program at its size, and the levels nest: each adds to the one below it rather than replacing it.
Base
$1.2Ma year
Base, about $1.2 million a year. The minimum at which the program in this document functions: the six rooms with travel support, partner agreements with the organizations in Section 6, Cortex.AI House, the cohort and technology pilots, a program coordinator, a minimal office, and the brand and communications work that generates the awareness the next round of funding depends on. Program leadership is inside the number. The base is designed to hold at that level across the three years; growth lives in the levels above it.
Mid
$2.1–2.3Ma year
Mid, about $2.1 to $2.3 million a year from Year Three, ramping from late Year Two. The first joint raise: a program director and an events coordinator, the full founder cohort and the emerging-GP accelerator, the LP peer network, more rooms, and the technology layer built out from the pilot.
Full
$3.1–3.8Ma year
Full, $3.1 to $3.8 million a year from Year Three, ramping from late Year Two. The staff complement, GP back-office support, non-dilutive founder grants, and a reserve for the physical-home decision in Year Three.
The Base is the first fundraising conversation, and it is the one this document is written to open.
The levels are built on a level commitment, the same amount each year; front-loaded or back-loaded structures, with performance triggers tied to the metrics in Section 8, can be modeled on the same lines. What the levels show is the optionality in the strategy: it scales up or down with the resources behind it. The initial commitment determines the next steps. Either the program runs at the Base from the start, or it fundraises immediately and operates as a special forces unit while the funding is developed, likely from the same foundations we hope will show up as LPs in the future.
Figures at every level exclude the Detroit Future City fiduciary fee, which Song Foundation carries above the commitment. Staff lines at the Mid and Full levels carry standard burden.

12  ·  What we hold to
Relentless about the outcome. Open about the how.
Everything in this document is done with the explicit intention of creating more equitable pathways into entrepreneurship and innovation for Black and brown founders, funders, and financiers. Alongside that intention, four things hold.
Widen the tent.
The Detroit ecosystem is capital-deficient regardless of race. As the program builds, coordination and collaboration with non-minority actors is both necessary and valuable. Where the program is unapologetic, it is also intentional about widening the tent, especially where the region's natural strengths are concerned.
Increase representation; don't build a tent just for us.
The broader trends in technology present unique opportunities for minority founders because of the cultural currency and communities they represent, but the trend extends far beyond minority communities. The regional implications, especially those that leverage manufacturing, hard-tech, physical AI, and climate-tech, are spaces where the program's value is to increase representation, not to singularly present and platform minority actors. It would be net-negative to build a tent just for us instead of a bigger one.
Capital influence is the lever.
Aggregating capital at this scale makes the program a necessary consideration in the region, and that is leverage. Some of the biggest gains will come downstream, because of the network's collective capital influence rather than from any single program. It is what allows the program to widen the tent and increase representation rather than ask for either.
Commit to the outcome, not the steps.
The project will evolve meaningfully as new information, matured perspectives, and new partners come to the table or emerge in the ecosystems where it operates. The commitment is to the outcome sought, not to the granular steps planned. Constant evaluation. Constant iteration and improvement.
